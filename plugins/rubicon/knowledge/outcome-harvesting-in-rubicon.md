# Outcome harvesting in Rubicon

Where the method maps onto a workflow of the six pieces (sample, code, group, tabulate, judge, write), where it does not, and what the workflow must say it cannot answer.

Back to [outcome harvesting](outcome-harvesting.md).

## As a plan

A harvest from documents is a drafting step, a grouping step, counts and a written answer. Of the method's six steps, the workflow can carry out drafting from documents (step two) and analysis (step five), and can report in the form step six asks for. Engaging change agents (step three) and substantiation (step four) are conversations with people, so they happen outside the workflow; their results reach it only when they are typed up as documents in the corpus. Designing the harvest with its users (step one) comes before the reading, and interpreting the findings (the second half of step five) and using them (step six) belong to the harvest users, so what the workflow gives them is candidate statements, definitions, tables and quotations to correct and argue with, not a finished harvest. A Rubicon run can stop at agreed points for people to take part: once the column definitions are written and a few documents coded, so that change agents and harvest users can agree what each value means before everything is counted, and once the answer is drafted, which is a draft to make sense of with them before it is final.

**Code.** One code step drafts candidate outcome statements from every document, one row per actor and per act, with the passage describing the change as the quotation. The coder sees only its prompt and the column definitions, so the prompt must carry the method's test in full: a named social actor doing something differently, already done rather than intended; what counts (formal acts, practices, relationships, resource decisions, a public position where the statement is the act); what does not (activities and outputs, awareness and attitude claims without an act, intentions, praise, changes without an actor); and the instruction to look for negative, mixed and unplanned changes as carefully as positive ones. Put the four-part statement in one free-text column (actor, change, where and when if stated) and give a nominal column to each field the analysis will count by: direction, whether the speaker frames the change as aimed for or unplanned, the kind of contribution claimed, and whose account it is (the actor's own, the programme's, or a third party's). Ask only for the columns the answer needs, since each extra column costs accuracy on the rest. Define each value tightly enough that another coder would place the same passage the same way, and where a passage sits near a boundary, say so in the row.

**Group.** Group the statement column into kinds of change, define each kind, and record each row's kind as a nominal column (or one binary column per kind where a statement may carry several). Keep an act apart from an activity, as [identifying outcome statements](outcome-harvesting-identifying.md) sets out, and group by kind of change rather than by programme objective. Where the evaluator supplies the programme's objectives, map the kinds to them after grouping, never before, so that unpredicted outcomes are not filtered out. Counts from an open coding are floors, because an open coder names the most obvious change and stops.

**Tabulate.** Count documents, not statements: how many documents yield at least one outcome, out of how many read, by whatever attribute says who is speaking; each kind of change by document; direction against framing. A count of statements describes the set and is never a measure of how much changed. There is no share of "all outcomes", because a harvest does not have a natural denominator; a proportion needs a declared frame, such as the list of funded partners, covered exhaustively.

**Judge.** Only where the evaluator gives a standard for "to what extent". Without one, leave the judge out and offer standards in the evaluator's terms.

**Write.** The answer follows the main page's reporting rules and the [analysis page](outcome-harvesting-analysis.md). Coverage first (what was read, what yielded outcomes, that nothing has been substantiated by an independent source if nothing has), then the kinds of change with quotations naming the actor, then the negative and unplanned outcomes in full, with contribution reported as a claim attached to whoever made it. A wholly positive set is reported as a finding about the search, not passed over.

**A trial.** A sample step drawing a handful of documents gives a first run the evaluator reads before the whole corpus is coded; it is the cheap place to catch a prompt that codes activities as outcomes, or change and attribution together.

## What the workflow must say it cannot answer

- Whether the programme caused an outcome, or whether it would have happened anyway: a harvest records what speakers say the programme did. Offer contribution analysis or process tracing on the few outcomes that carry the evaluation.
- Substantiation, unless the substantiators' replies are among the documents.
- "Intended" against a results framework, unless the framework is among the documents or given by the evaluator; otherwise it is the speaker's framing and is said to be.
- Any count with the actor as its case. A tabulation counts documents or rows; grouping an actor column can give each row an actor, but an actor does not have a base of its own.
