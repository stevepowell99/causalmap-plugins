# Process tracing in Rubicon

How process tracing maps onto a workflow: the register of predictions as column definitions fixed before any reading, matched codings for the mechanism and its rivals, and a searched-and-absent result kept apart from one never searched. A workflow written before the reading can show the method's central claim, that the predictions came first, and its most informative result, a prediction that failed, takes the most care to record.

Back to [process tracing](process-tracing.md).

## As a plan

A prediction is a value of a code step's column, defined before any document is read: a nominal column with a value for present and a value for absent, each with what would be found and where, what counts and what does not as a trace meeting the standard, and where a near miss goes instead; a binary column serves where no near miss needs its own definition. The evaluator reads this register, and any later tightening, before any document is read. A prediction written from what turned up in the material, rather than from the mechanism and the rivals alone, is exactly the ordering fault the method exists to rule out, so the register stands before the sample is read.

Each prediction, and each rival's prediction, gets its own column, coded over the same sample of documents with the same `section_chars`, so that a thinner search of a rival does not inflate the mechanism's case by default. Write each definition tightly enough that another careful coder would place the same passage the same way; where a passage sits close to the boundary between present and a near miss, the row says so, and the answer reports how many of a prediction's placements were close.

Tabulating each prediction's column gives the count for present and for absent out of the documents the sample read, so an absent prediction is reported with its base rather than left out; a document outside the sample never enters either count. The distinction the method asks for, between never searched and searched-and-absent, rests on the sample's document list being the base rather than the corpus.

Weighing the tabulated counts into a verdict on the mechanism is the write step's synthesis: every claim about a prediction points at the rows that carry it or at the tabulation cell stating its count. Where the evaluator wants the likelihood-ratio version, the counts are the input and the reasoning is written out in the answer's own words, with the independence of the evidence named rather than assumed, since two quotes from the same office are one piece of evidence and nothing in the counts says so on its own. The answer states the strongest evidence against its own conclusion and what would have changed it, as the method calls for.

Under `cannot_answer` the workflow names what counting cannot settle: whether a trace absent from the documents would have been created and would have survived, which is a claim about the record-keeping the evaluator has to make, and any part of the mechanism whose discriminating evidence lies outside the documents read. Trying the register on a handful of documents first catches, cheaply, a prediction written too vaguely to code consistently.
