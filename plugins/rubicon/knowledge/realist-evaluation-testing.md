# Realist evaluation: testing and refining

The cycle the method actually is, the interview technique it depends on, and what counts as a configuration surviving.

Back to [realist evaluation](realist-evaluation.md).

## The cycle

Realist evaluation is theory, test, refine, test again. Reports that show only the final configurations have presented the last quarter of the work as the whole of it.

**Round one: elicit.** Get the initial programme theory out of documents, out of implementers, and out of whatever literature covers this family of programmes. Implementers rarely state it as a theory. They state activities. The theory has to be reconstructed by asking why each activity was expected to lead anywhere.

**Round one: express.** Write candidate configurations, each capable of being wrong. Expect most of them to be poor. A first set that reads well is usually a set nobody could refute.

**Round two: test.** Design the data collection around the configurations. Who would know whether that reasoning happened? Where would the enabling context feature be visible? What would you see if the configuration were false?

**Round two: refine.** Rewrite in light of what came back, recording what changed and why. This is where the evaluation's value accumulates. It is also the part reports omit.

**Round three: consolidate.** Put the refined set to new respondents, or to a site held back for the purpose, and see whether it holds. Where budget allows only two rounds, say plainly that the final configurations are refined rather than tested.

## Demi-regularities

Start from a pattern rather than from an effect. A demi-regularity, Lawson's term which Pawson took up, is a semi-predictable tendency in the outcome data: adoption in eight sites and not in five, take-up high among one group and low among another, an effect that appears in year two and not in year one.

It does not have to be statistically respectable. It has to be real enough to be worth explaining and uneven enough to have an explanation. A uniform result across every site tells you the mechanism fired everywhere or nowhere. Either way there is nothing to configure.

So look at the outcome data first, and pick the split most surprising given the programme theory. That surprise is where the theory is wrong, which is where the work is.

## The realist interview

The technique is what makes realist evaluation possible. Running the analysis over material gathered any other way is the commonest reason it fails.

The move, from Pawson and developed by Manzano, is the teacher-learner cycle. The researcher teaches the respondent the theory under test. The respondent, who knows the situation, teaches the researcher where the theory is wrong. So the interview becomes a conversation about a proposition rather than an extraction of experience.

In practice that means saying something like: "we think people took up the offer where they already trusted the supervisor, and did not where they did not. Does that match what you saw? Where does it break down?" The respondent then corrects a specific claim, which beats an open question about their experience.

Manzano's three interview types map onto the cycle. Early interviews glean theory and stay open. Middle interviews refine, putting emerging configurations to people. Late interviews consolidate, testing a nearly settled set. One interview guide used throughout is a sign the cycle is not happening.

Two consequences for a corpus somebody else collected. Conventional interviews carry plenty about context and outcomes and much less about reasoning, so mechanisms inferred from them are candidates rather than findings. Nobody in that corpus was ever given the chance to say the theory was wrong either, so the material can fail to support a configuration and cannot refute one. Say which of those you are doing.

## What counts as support, refutation and refinement

A configuration is supported where the reasoning it names is described by people in the contexts it specifies, and where the outcome follows there and not in the contexts it excludes. Both halves matter. A mechanism found everywhere, including where the configuration says it should not fire, has been shown to be unconditioned rather than supported.

A configuration is refuted where the outcome occurs without the mechanism, or the mechanism is described and the outcome does not follow, and neither has an account. Refutation is rare and valuable. The temptation to rescue a configuration by adding a context condition is the thing to watch.

Adding a condition is legitimate as refinement. It stops being legitimate once it is done to save a configuration from every piece of contrary evidence in turn. The test: was the new condition specified before the next round of data, and does it exclude anything? A condition that only ever accommodates is an epicycle.

**Report the refuted ones.** A realist evaluation presenting six configurations, all supported, has either been extraordinarily lucky in its first draft or retired what did not work without saying so. The retired ones carry as much information as the survivors. They say what the programme's own theory got wrong.

## Deviant cases

The site where the mechanism should have fired and did not is worth more attention than the sites that behaved. It either reveals a context feature nobody had named, or shows the mechanism was misdescribed.

Same for the reverse: the site with none of the enabling conditions where the outcome appeared anyway. That one usually means a second configuration exists that the analysis has not reached. Finding it is how the set becomes complete enough to be useful.

## Reporting the route

For each configuration, show the initial version, each subsequent version with its date and what prompted the change, and the final one. Show the evidence for and against at each stage. Show the configurations that were dropped.

That version trail is the whole claim to rigour. Anybody can produce a plausible configuration after reading the data. Showing that one was written first, tested, found wanting in a specific way and rewritten is what makes it a finding rather than a description. Written as a workflow, each version of the configurations is a code step of its own, kept beside the earlier ones rather than overwriting them, with a note of what prompted the change, so the record [carries this trail](realist-evaluation-in-rubicon.md). It does not prove that a version was closed before the evidence arrived. Nor does it run the rounds: putting each version to implementers and participants and hearing where it breaks happens between workflows, with people, and a workflow reads what each round produced once it is in the documents.
