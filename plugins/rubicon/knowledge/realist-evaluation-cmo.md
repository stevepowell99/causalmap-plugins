# Realist evaluation: CMO configurations

What a mechanism actually is, what a context actually is, and why nearly every published configuration gets at least one of them wrong.

Back to [realist evaluation](realist-evaluation.md).

## The configuration

A CMO configuration is a hypothesis: in context C, mechanism M fires, producing outcome O. The three are a package. Pull them apart into variables, ask which contexts predict which outcomes, and you are doing a different method with realist vocabulary attached.

The test of a configuration is whether it could be wrong. "In supportive environments, supportive processes produce positive results" cannot be. "Where clinic managers already meet monthly, a shared referral form is adopted because staff can raise problems with it in a forum that already exists, so referral accuracy improves within two quarters; where no such forum exists the form is filled in and never discussed" can be, and each of its three parts names something checkable.

## A mechanism is a resource plus reasoning

This is the definition everything else turns on, and the one most often mangled.

A mechanism is not the programme's activity. Training is not a mechanism. Mentoring is not a mechanism. Providing a helpline is not a mechanism. Those are resources the programme introduces. A resource on its own causes nothing.

The mechanism is what participants do with the resource in their heads. Dalkin and colleagues put it as resource plus reasoning, and the split is worth keeping explicit when writing a configuration: the programme offered X; recipients, being in this situation, reasoned Y about it; therefore Z. Their reasoning is where the causal power sits. That is why the same offer produces different results among different people in the same room.

Three tests for a candidate mechanism.

**Could the programme have done it without anybody thinking anything?** If yes, you have named an activity.

**Does it name a response rather than a provision?** "Supervisors gained confidence to challenge senior clinicians" is a response. "Supervisors received coaching" is a provision.

**Would a participant recognise it as a description of their own reasoning?** If the sentence would puzzle the person it describes, it is probably the evaluator's theory about them rather than an account of what they did. The real test is asking them; a reading of what they said, which is all a workflow over documents can do, leaves a mechanism a candidate until participants have been asked.

Mechanisms are also usually invisible. They are inferred from what can be seen rather than observed directly. That is what makes [retroduction](#retroduction) the method's characteristic inferential move, and what makes access to participants' own accounts so important.

## Context is what switches the mechanism

Context is not the setting. Rural or urban, Zambia or Malawi, large clinic or small: those are labels for places. A configuration built on them says the programme works in some places and not others, which nobody needed an evaluation to learn.

Context is the feature of a situation that lets the mechanism fire or stops it. Pre-existing relationships. Whether a forum already exists. Whether staff have discretion. Whether managers are rewarded for the outcome or for something else. Prior experience of a similar programme that went badly. Norms about who may question whom.

The useful discipline: for each candidate context feature, say how it enables or blocks the specific reasoning in the mechanism. Where you cannot say that, the feature is background rather than context. "In rural districts" becomes "where staff travel two hours to a meeting, so a monthly forum meets only when fuel is available". The second version explains something the first only labels.

Context also operates at several levels at once, and naming which one is worth the trouble. The individual's history, the team's relationships, the organisation's rules, the wider system's incentives. A configuration that puts everything at one level usually misses where the switch actually sits.

## Outcomes, in respects

The full question is "in what respects", which means outcomes are plural and often pull against each other. A mechanism can improve one thing and damage another. A configuration reporting only the intended outcome stopped looking early.

Outcomes also come at different distances from the mechanism. Immediate: staff raise problems in the forum. Intermediate: the referral form is revised. Distal: referral accuracy improves. Naming the distance a configuration reaches keeps it from claiming more than it tested.

## Middle-range theory

Pawson's requirement, and the thing that separates realist evaluation from a well-organised case study. A configuration should be an instance of something more abstract that other programmes could also instantiate.

The referral example above, abstracted: a new procedure is adopted where an existing routine forum lets its users renegotiate it, and is complied with formally where no such forum exists. That is a claim about procedures and forums rather than about referral forms in this district. Somebody evaluating a different programme could test it.

Two practical moves. Ask, for each configuration, what family of programmes it would apply to. And go and look for the existing theory. The mechanism you have just discovered has very often been described somewhere as diffusion, or as street-level bureaucracy, or as psychological safety, and connecting to it is what makes the finding usable.

Where the abstraction cannot be made, say so. A local configuration labelled as local is more use than a spurious generalisation.

## Retroduction

The inferential move realist evaluation runs on, and the one hardest to describe as a procedure. From an observed pattern, ask what must be true, and what hidden thing must have been operating, for this pattern to have come about.

It is neither induction nor deduction. Induction would generalise from the cases to a pattern. Deduction would derive predictions from a theory. Retroduction goes from the pattern to a candidate explanation of a kind nobody can observe, then puts the candidate to the test.

In practice it looks like this. The outcome data show adoption in eight sites and formal compliance in five. Ask what could produce that split, generating several candidates rather than one. Then ask what each implies you would find in the material if it were right, and what you would find if it were wrong. Then go and look.

The discipline that keeps retroduction from being invention: generate more than one candidate, write down what would distinguish them before looking, and report the candidates that lost. Everything else in it is imagination. That is legitimate, and it is where the method gets its reach.

## What to record per configuration

The context features, each with a sentence on how it enables or blocks the mechanism. The resource the programme offered. The reasoning it is hypothesised to trigger, in terms a participant would recognise. The outcomes, in respects, at named distances. The middle-range theory it instantiates. The evidence supporting it and the evidence against. Its version history, since a configuration that never changed during a realist evaluation was probably never tested.
