# Rubrics and verdicts

Description says what the documents show; valuing says how good that is. The value is not in the material. Looking at it cannot tell you where adequate begins, and a standard chosen after seeing the numbers can be made to fit any of them. So the standard comes from a person, in advance and under their name, and the work applies it in the open.

## Getting the rubric

- Ask the evaluator for their rubric, or for the document that holds one: terms of reference, an evaluation framework, the programme's targets. Quote it verbatim in `record/plan.md`.
- Without one, ask for it, offering two or three standards in the evaluator's terms as forms without numbers: "supporting cases outnumber contradicting ones by at least ___ to one"; "every link in the chain supported by at least ___ cases". Meanwhile plan up to the table the verdict would read. Told to go ahead without a standard, deliver the table and say in one sentence that the verdict waits on theirs. Never set a provisional standard of your own, however plainly it is labelled.
- A margin between two things is a standard, and so is a yardstick the question names ("against the intended outcomes"): ask for it or for its document.

## What a rubric holds

Write it out in the plan and in the answer:

- **Verdicts**, listed best first, each saying what it stands for. "Green" alone is not a standard; "every partner described a change they could date and name a cause for" is.
- **Criteria**, each a question with its own levels, each level with a descriptor of what earns it.
- **A rule or a reading for each criterion.** A criterion that turns on a number is a rule over a saved count, applied by code and never left to a model's reading of the numbers. A criterion that cannot be counted is a reading, weighing quoted passages against the descriptors and citing them.
- **How criteria combine**: all must hold, none may fail, the weakest decides (a chain, such as a theory of change that fails at its weakest link), or the best decides (two routes to one outcome); or each criterion is rated on a scale of its own and the evaluator's rules say which combinations give which verdict, tried in order ("right where reach is at least adequate and quality is not poor; otherwise not right"). Never average ordinal levels, since an average hides the failure in one place. A rubric that keeps its criteria apart reports each and says they were not combined.
- **When no verdict can be given.**

## Applying a rule

Make each rule test exactly what the standard says: the same quantity (people where they said people, a share of a base where they said a share), over the base they named, at their threshold, giving the verdicts they named. A rule that tests something nearby is a standard of your own. A standard that promises a ratio needs both sides counted: "at least three supporters, and supporters outnumber contradictors two to one" is two tests, and the first alone would rate a link with twelve for and six against as well supported.

Take the numbers from `mcp__rubicon__count`, try the levels strongest first, and show the whole comparison in the answer: both counts, the threshold, and the level the first rule met gives. "Supporting [[count: link1_for]], contradicting [[count: link1_against]]: at least twice as many support as contradict, so the link is strong on the evaluator's standard."

## Applying a reading

Brief a subagent (an `adjudicator`, or one of your own design on Opus) with the criterion, its levels and descriptors, and the cells or mentions that bear on it. It gives a level, cites the quotations it relied on, names the evidence that pulls the other way, and says where the evidence is too thin to place. A criterion about the method rather than the material, such as whether contrary evidence was looked for, cites the record rather than quotations. A verdict for each case follows the same rules over that case's cells, with the rule stated and each case's level listed.

## When no verdict can be given

The material shows the question assumes something that did not happen; it compares things the documents cannot compare fairly; the thing was never searched for; or too few documents bear on it. Report that, with the evidence, instead of a level. A criterion may refuse the question.

## The table beside the verdict

A verdict compresses the analysis into a word that tracing back does not rebuild. Beside every verdict, give the table it read: for each criterion, the counts, the cases, the key quotations and the level. Where the evaluator does not want criteria settled before the reading, stop at the table.

## Traps

- A verdict name that travels further than its description. Where the weakest criterion decides, the overall verdict carries the weakest criterion's name, so name verdicts for the level rather than for one criterion's failing.
- A verdict nobody asked for, which becomes the line that gets quoted without its qualifications.
- Ethics, or transferability to another setting, scored from transcripts: both lie outside what the documents show.
